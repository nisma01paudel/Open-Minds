## The same data at observation level

The table above counts **sites**: 125 of 142 had at least one usable look. That is the
question "could we have said *anything* about this slope?". The other question is "how
much could we see?", and it has a different denominator — every scene screened.

Across **142 sites, 2,021 scenes, 676 of them usable**:

| Calendar month (pooled over 37 year-months, 2018-06 to 2026-09) | Scenes | Usable | Observation-level usability |
|---|---|---|---|
| June | 415 | 223 | 53.7% |
| July | 518 | 169 | 32.6% |
| August | 518 | 87 | 16.8% |
| September | 403 | 112 | 27.8% |
| October | 167 | 85 | 50.9% |

**Observation-level usability is 33.4% overall; August is 16.8%.** A third of the
imagery that exists is usable, and in the worst month a sixth.

Both denominators matter and neither replaces the other:

- **88% of sites** had something usable → the system is rarely completely deaf, which is why it does not simply refuse everything.
- **12% of sites (17 slopes)** had *nothing* usable in the month before they failed → for those, silence would be indistinguishable from safety unless the system says so.
- **Median days since the last usable look: 8; but 29 sites were over two weeks stale**, and the maximum is the full 30-day window.

That last figure is the operational one. A monitoring system that reports "the last
usable look was 27 days ago" is telling an inspector something the imagery alone cannot.

**This is still not a skill claim.** It measures whether the evidence required to say
anything existed at all - the precondition for any downstream warning, and the honest
ceiling on what an optical system could have done.
